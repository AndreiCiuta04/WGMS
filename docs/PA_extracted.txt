2 Project Description
2.1 Initial Situation & Existing Similar Products
Initial Situation and Sponsor Pain Points
A typical shared flat (Wohngemeinschaft, WG) in Austria or Germany operates as a small
informal organisation: rent and utilities are pooled, groceries are bought ad hoc by whoever
is at the supermarket, chores rotate by spoken or hand-written agreement, and the financial
liability for the household is concentrated on a single main tenant (Hauptmieter*in) who holds
the lease. The tooling that supports this organisation, however, is fragmented across at least
four parallel channels: a WhatsApp group for communication, a printed schedule on the fridge
for chores, an expense-splitting app such as Splitwise for receipts, and the memory of the
main tenant for everything else. Receipts disappear into chat history, paper schedules decay
within weeks, and verbal agreements are forgotten between the people who made them and
the people who join the household later.
The recurring failure modes of this arrangement constitute the sponsor pain points the WGMS
project commits to address:
•Financial leakage on the main tenant.Micro-expenses below approximately five euros
are systematically not reclaimed because the social cost of asking exceeds the monetary
value. Over an eleven-month academic year, the cumulative under-recovery is non-trivial
and is borne entirely by the lease holder.
•Unbounded coordination overhead.MonthlyKassensturz(manual reconciliation) re-
quires the main tenant to scroll weeks of WhatsApp history, cross-reference receipts, and
individually chase three to five roommates. The opportunity cost is several hours of work
per month that produces no output beyond restoring an accurate balance.
•Liability without authority.The main tenant carries legal responsibility toward the land-
lord and economic responsibility toward third parties, but has no enforceable mechanism
to ensure that roommates discharge their share. Disputes therefore escalate to the level
of personal relationships rather than being resolved at the tooling layer.
•Onboarding gap on roommate turnover.When a roommate joins or leaves mid-term,
the verbal and paper-based record of the household state cannot be transferred. The
new roommate inherits an opaque set of conventions and is routinely surprised by their
first overdue chore or first unexpected expense, eroding goodwill from day one.
3
Existing Similar Products
Three categories of solution currently address fragments of the problem above. None ad-
dresses it as a whole.
Splitwiseis the dominant English-language expense-splitting service, available on the web,
iOS, and Android. Its strength is a mature settlement model that handles unequal splits, multi-
ple currencies, and partial payments. With respect to the WG use case it is purely a financial
ledger: it has no concept of recurring chores, shopping lists, household membership, or shared
communication. WGs that adopt Splitwise must continue to maintain WhatsApp, paper sched-
ules, and any shopping coordination outside the tool.
Flatastic(also marketed asDie WG-App) is the closest direct competitor in the German-
speaking market. It is a mobile-first application that bundles four modules: a cleaning schedule
with a points-based ranking system, a synchronised shopping list, a shared expense ledger,
and a pinboard calledShoutsfor lightweight intra-WG communication. Flatastic is offered as a
freemium product with a subscription that unlocks features such as expense export, photo at-
tachments on receipts, and per-item shopping notes. Flatastic has a substantial installed base
and a long product history, but is constrained in three ways relevant to WGMS. First, its primary
surface is mobile, which excludes desktop-first administrative workflows. Second, it treats ev-
ery household uniformly — there is no domain-level distinction between a financially-liable main
tenant and an ordinary roommate. Third, it has no concept of inter-WG interaction.
Splidrepresents a third class of competitor: lightweight, single-purpose group expense track-
ers (alongside Tricount and similar apps). These are excellent for tracking one trip or one project
but do not provide the persistent, role-aware household model that a long-lived WG requires.
Status quo: WhatsApp and paper.The actual incumbent that WGMS must displace is the
combination of a WhatsApp group, a fridge-mounted cleaning schedule, and the ad-hoc mem-
ory of whoever paid last. This combination has the lowest possible onboarding cost (zero) and
the highest possible familiarity. Any specialised tool must demonstrably outperform it on the
workflows that matter most — adding an expense, viewing the current balance, and confirming
that a chore is done — to be adopted at all.

Identified Gap and Unique Selling Propositions
Table 1 identifies a concrete market gap: no existing product offers a role-aware, web-first,
integrated WG operating system. WGMS positions itself against this gap through five unique
selling propositions:
•Single source of truth for the entire WG lifecycle.Expenses, chores, shopping, com-
munication, and appliance usage live in one persistent application with one coherent do-
main model. The main tenant gains a single dashboard that answers “what is open?”
without context-switching between four tools.
•Role-aware household with seamless onboarding.WGMS is the only system in the
comparison that distinguishes the financially-liable Main Tenant from ordinary Room-
mates as first-class domain concepts, enforced at the API layer through role-based ac-
cess control. Onboarding via time-limited invitation links transfers the entire household
state to a new roommate at the moment they join, eliminating the “opaque inheritance”
failure mode described above.
•Deterministic settlement engine.Cascading debts are reduced via a minimum-transactions
debt-graph algorithm executed inside a Spring@Transactionalboundary usingBigDecimal
arithmetic with fixed HALF_UP rounding to two decimal places. This eliminates the
floating-point drift that plagues hand-rolled spreadsheet settlements and is verifiable through
unit tests, which makes it directly gradeable.
•Passive cross-platform NFC appliance logging.NFC tags are encoded as deep-link
URLs that the mobile OS resolves natively into the Angular SPA — no Web NFC API
required. The user taps an appliance and the application opens on the corresponding
logging view. This works identically on iOS Safari (which does not expose Web NFC) and
on Android, and is the project’s most distinctive non-CRUD feature.
•Cross-WG social layer.The optional event and feed module allows registered WGs to
interact with one another — inviting another flat to a party, following a friend’s WG. No
competitor offers this, and it is the one module that creates a network effect rather than a
closed-loop tool.
Business Model and Monetisation Paths
The Project Contract scope does not include payment processing, ad serving, or B2B contract-
ing. The architecture is, however, designed to be compatible with the following commercial
paths, recorded here to motivate design decisions (the role model, the optional modules, the
GDPR-aligned data handling) without expanding the implementation scope. None of these
paths produces a deliverable in this contract or influences grading.
•Freemium with non-invasive advertising.The core workflows — registration, WG cre-
ation, expenses, chores, shopping, dashboard — remain free and ad-supported through
placements modelled on the New Y ork Times Games approach: a single static banner per
session, no interstitials, no interruption of task flows. A small subset of quality-of-life fea-
tures (e.g. data export, advanced filters) may later move behind an ad-free subscription.
The workflows that drive adoption are never paywalled, ensuring parity with the zero-cost
incumbent (WhatsApp + paper).
•Micro-fee on settlement transactions.A small fixed fee in the order of one to five euro
cents per recorded settlement could be levied to monetise the deterministic settlement
engine without burdening micro-expenses themselves. The fee would be capped per
5
month and waived in the freemium tier above a configurable threshold, so users who
settle once a month pay nothing measurable.
•B2B partnership with property management firms (Immobilienverwaltungen).Prop-
erty management firms that lease student housing inventory absorb the indirect cost of
tenant disputes through support overhead, escalations to the landlord, and reduced reten-
tion. A licensing arrangement allows such firms to offer WGMS as a pre-installed amenity
for their tenants, with the firm paying a per-WG fee and the residents receiving the ad-free
experience by default. The partnership also opens two firm-side workflows that the exist-
ing data model can support without migration: residents reporting damages through the
app (linked to the household and the appliance domain), and the firm dispatching invoices
to the WG through the same interface used for shared expenses. A future portfolio role
for the firm would be a strictly read-only aggregate view across its WGs — no leakage
between flats.
•Student-targeted sponsorships.A WG is, in aggregate, a small commercial cohort
with a predictable life cycle. Targeted sponsorship slots could be offered to partners
whose products fit that cohort: banks promoting student accounts, local event services
such as Goodnight.at or Freyzeit.at, and the kind of merchant offers found in printed
Gutscheinhefte. Sponsorship slots would be served through the same banner placements
as the freemium tier, never inside task flows, and would be configurable per WG so that
households can opt out of categories they find irrelevant.
All four paths are listed in Section 6.4 as commercial options that the system is designed to
not preclude. The role-aware data model, the household-scoped privacy boundary, and the
GDPR-aligned data handling are the technical preconditions for any of them.
2.2 Project Overview & Technical SE Goals
Project Overview
WGMS is a responsive, browser-based information system that consolidates the operational
coordination of a shared household into a single application. It is delivered as an Angular Single
Page Application backed by a Java Spring Boot REST API and an embedded H2 relational
database, deployed via GitLab CI/CD to the university’s Kubernetes cluster (the technology
stack is fully specified in Section 3.1). Functionally, the system is organised around seven use
case packages (UC-01 to UC-07, Section 4.1) covering registration and household creation,
task and cleaning management, expense tracking and settlement, collaborative shopping, intra-
and inter-WG communication and events, the central dashboard, and the optional NFC-based
appliance log.
The product is intentionally scoped as aweb application only. No native iOS or Android clients
are produced. Cross-device compatibility is achieved through a responsive design tested on
viewports between 360 pixels and 1920 pixels in width, and through the passive NFC NDEF
URL routing strategy described in Section 3.2, which avoids any platform-specific NFC API. The
system handles real shared-household data (names, email addresses, expenses, messages,
appliance usage records) but does not execute monetary transactions, integrate with banking
systems, or process special categories of personal data under the GDPR.
Technical Software Engineering Goals
The Project Contract commits the team to the following measurable technical goals. Each
goal is phrased so that fulfilment can be assessed directly from the GitLab repository and the
6
deployed artefact, in line with the assessment criteria communicated by the SEPR teaching
team.
•TG-01 — Three-tier architecture.A clean separation between Angular SPA, Spring
Boot REST API, and persistence layer (Spring Data JPA over H2). No business logic in
the frontend; no database access outside repositories.
•TG-02 — Authentication and authorisation.Stateless authentication via JSON Web
Tokens, password hashing via BCrypt, and role-based access control enforced on the
backend (not only hidden in the frontend). Two roles are distinguished at the domain
level:Main T enantandRoommate.
•TG-03 — Deterministic financial logic.The settlement engine reduces the inter-roommate
debt graph to its minimum-transactions form usingjava.math.BigDecimalwith HALF_UP
rounding to two decimal places, executed within a Spring@Transactionalservice bound-
ary. Settlement correctness is verified by unit tests covering equal splits, unequal splits,
rounding edge cases, deleted-user fallbacks, and partially-settled payments (NFR-07,
NFR-10).
•TG-04 — API documentation and contracts.All REST endpoints are described by an
OpenAPI (Swagger) specification served by the running application. The Angular client
consumes this contract, and integration tests verify that documented and actual behaviour
agree.
•TG-05 — Continuous integration and deployment.Every push to a feature branch
triggers a GitLab pipeline that builds the project, runs the full test suite, and (on the main
branch) builds a Docker image, pushes it to the project registry, and deploys it to the
university Kubernetes cluster. No deployment artefact is produced manually.
•TG-06 — Cross-platform NFC integration without a native app.Physical NFC tags
are encoded as deep-link URLs pointing to specific appliance routes in the Angular SPA.
The integration is verified to work on iOS Safari (where the Web NFC API is not exposed)
and on Chromium-based Android browsers. A QR-code fallback is provided for devices
without NFC support, satisfying NFR-12.
•TG-07 — GDPR-aligned data handling.The system supports user-initiated account
deletion or anonymisation, a data export endpoint for self-service access, time-limited in-
vitation tokens, and an explicit data inventory documented in Section 10.1. No production
secrets are committed to the repository.
•TG-08 — Browser and viewport support.The application targets the current and pre-
vious major releases of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge as of project start, on desktop
and mobile viewports between 360 and 1920 pixels wide. Browser support is recorded in
the README and verified through cross-browser smoke tests before each milestone.
•TG-09 — Test coverage on critical logic.Backend services classified as critical (au-
thentication, settlement engine, task assignment, invitation handling) are covered by au-
tomated tests. The Quality Assurance Strategy (Section 9.2) defines the merge criteria.
•TG-10 — Maintainability and handover.The repository contains a project README, an
architecture overview consistent with the C4 diagrams in Section 3.3, and documentation
for every public module. The handover package follows the deliverables list in Section 7.

3 Asset Architecture & System Environment
An asset is defined as any component, framework, or infrastructure that contributes value to the
information system. The WG Management System (WGMS) architecture follows the provided
university baseline and extends it with domain-specific modules for household management,
NFC usage logging, voice-assisted automation, and LLM-based command parsing.
3.1 Asset Network & Infrastructure Stack
To fulfill the domain requirements while adhering to the technical directives, the project uses
the following technology stack:
9
•Client-Side (Frontend):The system is implemented as a responsive Single Page Appli-
cation (SPA) usingAngular 21.x. Node.js24.x LTSis used for package management,
development tooling, and production builds. The UI is optimized for the defined reference
devices and browser versions listed in the compatibility section. The goal is not pixel-
perfect support for every device, but a stable and usable experience on the agreed target
platforms.
•Server-Side (Backend):The backend is aJava Spring Boot 4.0.xREST API targeting
OpenJDK 26in the reference setup. It usesSpring Securityfor Role-Based Access
Control (RBAC) and authentication via JSON Web Tokens. It also usesMapStructfor
DTO mapping andOpenAPI/Swaggerfor interactive API documentation.
•Data Persistence:For the university project scope, the system uses an embedded
H2 Database 2.4.xin automatic mixed mode. Data access is abstracted usingSpring
Data JPAand the Jakarta Persistence API (JPA). In deployed demo environments, the
database file should either be stored on a persistent volume or recreated through doc-
umented seed data. WGMS does not claim production-grade database durability with
embedded H2. A migration path to PostgreSQL is documented as future work.
•Infrastructure & CI/CD:The project usesGitLab CI/CD. After a successful pipeline run,
the deployment job builds a Docker image, pushes it to the project registry, and deploys it
to the university’sKubernetes Clusterwhere applicable. If the deployment environment
is limited, the minimum deliverable is a reproducible local Docker setup and documented
deployment instructions.
•Optional AI-powered ASR System:Voice-assisted automation is treated as an ex-
tended feature. The architecture reserves an optional integration point for a standalone
Whisper-Flowcontainer based on Python/FastAPI. If implemented, the Angular frontend
streams raw audio via WebSockets to this service. The service performs in-memory tran-
scription using local Whisper models and returns text to the Spring Boot backend for
command parsing and validation. If time or device constraints prevent real-time audio im-
plementation, written natural-language commands may be delivered first, while real-time
speech transcription remains future work.
•Optional Google Cloud Vertex AI Gemini Command Parser:ASR output or typed
natural-language commands are parsed by a backend-only Google Cloud Vertex AI /
Gemini integration. The reference model isgemini-3-flash-preview(Gemini 3 Flash).
Because Gemini 3 Flash is currently provided as a preview model, the exact model ID
is kept configurable in the backend environment configuration. The LLM is limited to
command-to-JSON parsing, receives only minimal context, and never executes actions di-
rectly. Backend validation, permission checks, and user confirmation remain the planned
safety mechanism.
3.2 Gap & Risk Analysis
Because WGMS is developed on top of the provided baseline, the implementation gap consists
of extending the existing vertical slice with WG-specific domain logic.
The first major challenge is theCross-Platform NFC Integration. Bridging physical household
appliances to a web application is technically complex because iOS Safari does not support the
active Web NFC API through JavaScript. To mitigate this constraint and avoid a native mobile
app, the architecture usesPassive NDEF URL Routing. NFC tags are encoded with deep-link
URLs pointing to specific application resources. When a user taps a smartphone against an
appliance tag, the operating system opens the corresponding WGMS view where supported.
10
This feature remains optional and should not block the core WGMS workflow if device support
is limited.
The second critical risk area is the implementation of theSettlement Engine. Calculating
shared expenses and cascading debts between roommates requires precise decimal handling
and safe transaction boundaries. This risk will be addressed through Spring’s@Transactional
service layer and automated tests for the financial calculation logic.
The third risk involves theH2 Database Lifecycle. Since H2 may run in a local or containerized
environment, data persistence across restarts must be handled carefully. Test data, database
initialization, and migration scripts need to be documented to avoid accidental data loss. The
project does not claim production-grade database durability with embedded H2.
The fourth risk is theVoice-Assisted Automation Pipeline. Audio transcription and command
parsing may produce incorrect results. Therefore, the system restricts automation to predefined
intents, validates parsed JSON against a schema, and asks for confirmation before sensitive
changes such as expenses, task completion, appliance logging, or payment-related updates
are executed.
The fifth risk is theLLM Command Parser. Because LLM outputs can be incomplete, mal-
formed, or semantically wrong, the LLM is not allowed to directly execute system actions. It only
proposes a structured JSON command. The backend validates the JSON schema, checks user
permissions, verifies referenced entities, and asks for user confirmation for sensitive changes
before execution.

4.2 Scope and Priority Policy
To keep the project feasible within the available effort of 720 hours, the project follows a strict
priority policy.
Core functionalityis prioritized before extended functionality is implemented. The core scope
consists of registration and login, WG creation and invitations, task and cleaning schedule man-
agement, shared expense tracking, settlement overview, collaborative shopping management,
dashboard functionality, history views, security basics, and documentation.
Extended functionalityincludes NFC appliance usage tracking, voice-assisted automation,
Google Cloud Gemini command parsing, direct chat, cross-WG chat, public feed functionality,
stock prediction, fair chore balancing, and one-click expense generation. These features are
implemented only after the core workflow is stable. If time is limited, extended features may be
delivered as simplified prototypes, documented design concepts, or future-work items.
The system is considered successful if the core WG-management workflow is functional, tested,
documented, and demonstrable on the defined reference devices. Extended features improve
the final result but should not endanger the delivery of the core system.

5.1 Domain Model Explanation
The domain model describes the main data structure of the WG Management System. The
central entity isHousehold, which represents one shared flat and connects the main system ar-
eas: roommates, tasks, expenses, shopping, appliances, events, communication, notifications,
and automation.
ARoommatebelongs to a household and has a role such asMAIN_TENANTorROOMMATE. Tasks are
modeled withTaskandTaskAssignment, where assignments store the due date and status of
a task. The finance area consists ofExpense,ExpenseSplit, andPayment, allowing the system
to calculate balances and track settlements between roommates.
Shopping is represented byShoppingItem,ShoppingRequest, andShoppingSuggestion. Ap-
pliance usage is modeled throughAppliance,NfcTag,NfcScan, andApplianceUsage. Com-
munication and social features are represented byWGConversation,WGMessage,GroupChat,
DirectChat,Event,EventParticipation,FeedPost, andFollow.
The model also includes a natural-language automation layer. Users can enter or speak com-
mands such as “I bought milk” or “I used the air fryer”. Spoken commands are first tran-
scribed into text by the ASR service where implemented. The resulting text is stored as a
NaturalLanguageCommandobject and parsed by the Google Cloud Vertex AI Gemini parser into
a structuredParsedCommand. The system then createsAutomationActionobjects, which can
update shopping items, create expenses, log appliance usage, complete tasks, create events,
send messages, or trigger notifications.AutomationConfigdefines whether confirmation is
required before automated actions are executed.
Some features, such as the dashboard, history view, fair chore balancing, stock prediction, and
one-click expense generation, are modeled as derived behavior instead of separate entities
because they are calculated from existing domain data.

8.2 Quality Assurance Strategy
Quality assurance is integrated into the development process rather than treated as a final
project phase. The main goal is to detect defects early, especially in security-related workflows,
data consistency, expense calculation, LLM command parsing, and automation handling.
Testing levels:
•Unit tests:Used for backend services, especially expense splitting, payment settlement, task
assignment, invitation handling, notification logic, JSON schema validation, and automation
validation.
•Integration tests:Used to verify the interaction between the REST API, database, authenti-
cation layer, Whisper-Flow service, Google Cloud Vertex AI Gemini integration, and important
business workflows where implemented.
•Frontend tests:Used for important UI workflows such as registration, adding expenses,
completing tasks, viewing the dashboard, and confirming automation actions.
•Manual acceptance tests:Used before each milestone to check whether the implemented
features solve the intended WG use cases.
•Reference-device tests:Used to verify that core workflows remain usable on iPhone 12
Pro/iOS 26.x/Safari, Google Pixel 7/Android 16/Chrome 147.x+, and desktop browsers.
Review process:
• Every major feature should be implemented on a separate Git branch.

9 Legal, Compliance and External Hosting Constraints
WGMS processes personal data because users create accounts, join households, receive
tasks, record expenses, send messages, and may generate appliance usage, ASR transcripts,
LLM parser outputs, or automation records. Therefore, even though the project is developed
in a university context, privacy and data protection requirements must be considered from the
beginning.
9.1 Processed Data Categories
The system may process the following categories of data:
•Account data:Name, email address, password hash, authentication tokens, and user role.
•WG membership data:Household membership, invitation links, user roles such as Main
Tenant or Roommate, and join/leave timestamps.
•Task data:Assigned chores, completion status, due dates, and task history.
•Expense data:Shared expenses, paid amounts, participants, payment status, and settle-
ment history.
•Shopping data:Shopping items, requests, suggestions, votes, and generated expense ref-
erences.
•Communication data:Notifications, direct messages, group messages, WG conversations,
feed posts, and event-related messages.
•Appliance usage data:Appliance identifiers, NFC scan records, usage timestamps, and the
user who created the usage record.
•Voice and automation data:Temporary audio streams, transcribed commands, parsed in-
tents, structured JSON payloads, confirmation decisions, execution status, and timestamps.
•LLM parser data:User command text, allowed-intent prompts, limited context snapshots for
entity matching, parser output, confidence value, validation result, and user-facing summary.
•Technical data:Application logs, error messages, timestamps, and security-relevant events.
The project doesnotrequire processing of special categories of personal data such as health
data, religious beliefs, political opinions, or biometric data. The system also doesnotexecute
real money transfers and does not need to store bank account numbers, credit card data,
or payment provider credentials. Raw audio data should only be processed temporarily for
transcription and should not be stored permanently.
9.2 GDPR-Oriented Design Principles
The design of WGMS follows the most important GDPR-oriented principles:
•Purpose limitation:Data is collected only for clear WG management purposes such as
organizing chores, expenses, shopping, communication, shared resources, and confirmed
automation actions.
•Data minimization:Only data needed for the planned features is stored. Optional features
such as public feed posts, NFC appliance tracking, cross-WG communication, voice automa-
tion, or LLM parsing should not be activated by default if they are not necessary for the core
WG workflow.
30
•Transparency:Users should be informed which data is stored and why. A short privacy
notice should be included in the final documentation or application.
•Storage limitation:Data should not be kept longer than necessary. Invitation tokens should
expire automatically. Deleted accounts should be removed or anonymized where possible.
•Integrity and confidentiality:Personal data must be protected through authentication, au-
thorization, secure password handling, HTTPS in deployment, restricted database access,
careful log handling, and minimal LLM context sharing.
•Accountability:The team documents the main data categories, security measures, external
services, AI/LLM components, and known privacy risks.
9.3 User Rights and Data Handling
The system should support basic user rights as far as possible within the project scope:
•Access:A user should be able to view their account information, WG membership, assigned
tasks, expenses, communication records, appliance usage records, automation history, and
stored command-parser records where relevant.
•Correction:Incorrect profile, household, task, shopping, expense, or automation data should
be editable by the user or by an authorized WG administrator.
•Deletion:Users should be able to leave a WG and request account deletion. Where finan-
cial history must remain understandable for other roommates, the user can be anonymized
instead of fully deleting historical expense records.
•Export:If feasible, the system should provide a simple export of user-related data or allow
manual database export during the project demo.
9.4 External Hosting Constraints
If WGMS is deployed externally, the following constraints apply:
• Hosting should preferably take place on university-provided infrastructure or servers located
in the EU/EEA.
• If a third-party hosting provider or external AI provider is used, the provider should be docu-
mented as an external processor.
• The LLM parser uses Google Cloud Vertex AI / Gemini as an external AI service. The
provider, region, service account, model ID, prompt contents, and processed data categories
should be documented.
• If personal data is transferred outside the EU/EEA, the team should check whether appropri-
ate safeguards such as adequacy decisions or standard contractual clauses are required.
• Production secrets, database passwords, JWT secrets, and API keys must not be committed
to Git.
• Test environments should use artificial demo data instead of real personal data whenever
possible.
• Application logs should not contain passwords, tokens, private messages, raw audio, full
financial details, or unnecessary LLM prompts/responses.

9.5 Security Constraints
Security is especially important because the system combines personal communication, finan-
cial household records, automation commands, LLM parsing, and role-based WG administra-
tion. Therefore, the following constraints are defined:
• Passwords must be hashed and salted.
• Authentication tokens must expire after a reasonable time.
• Invitation links must be time-limited and difficult to guess.
• Users may only access data belonging to their own WG unless a feature explicitly supports
public cross-WG interaction.
• Main Tenant or administrator functions must be protected by role checks on the backend, not
only hidden in the frontend.
• NFC-based appliance usage must be transparent to users and should not be used for hidden
monitoring.
• Automation actions are validated on the backend before execution.
• Sensitive automation actions should require explicit user confirmation.
• LLM output must always be treated as untrusted input.
• The LLM parser should not receive authentication tokens, password hashes, private message
histories, or raw audio files.
• The Google Cloud Vertex AI integration should use restricted service-account credentials,
least-privilege IAM roles, and protected backend-only API access. Frontend clients should
not call the Gemini API directly.
9.6 Residual Compliance Risk
The main residual compliance risk is that WGMS can become more privacy-sensitive if optional
features such as public WG feeds, cross-WG communication, appliance usage tracking, voice-
assisted automation, or LLM-based command parsing are expanded. These features increase
the amount of behavioral, social, and inferred action data stored by the system. Therefore,
they should remain optional, limited in scope, and clearly separated from the core private WG
management functions.
For the current project scope, no real payment execution, no banking integration, no persistent
raw audio storage, a documented external Google Cloud Vertex AI / Gemini dependency for
command parsing, and no special-category personal data are planned. This keeps the compli-
ance risk manageable and appropriate for a university software engineering project.
